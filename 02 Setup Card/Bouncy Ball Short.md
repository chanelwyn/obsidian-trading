# Bouncy Ball Short
## Purpose
Short a trend continuation after an established backside downtrend. Price bounces into resistance, loses momentum, then resumes the primary trend.
Best used to join institutional selling rather than anticipate reversals.

---
## Qualification
### Market Context
- Daily bias = bearish
- Confirmed backside preferred
- Intraday downtrend established
- Bearish market regime or weak sector
- SSR inactive
- Adequate liquidity & borrow
### Required Conditions
- Prior down-leg exists (**mandatory**)
- PB into resistance (EMA / VWAP / prior support)
- PB on ↓Vol
- Clear LH
- Tight consolidation near resistance
- Room to next support ≥2R
### Checklist
- [ ] Daily bias short
- [ ] Prior down-leg confirmed
- [ ] Backside context
- [ ] PB ↓Vol
- [ ] LH confirmed
- [ ] Resistance identified
- [ ] Trigger level defined
- [ ] Risk defined before entry
---
## Execution
### Trigger
- Break < LH low
- BD of consolidation
- Tape Conf
- Seller aggression increasing
### Entry
- Enter on confirmed BD.
- Avoid anticipatory entries.
- Add only after successful continuation.
### Stop
Use nearest structural invalidation.
Examples:
- Above LH
- Above VWAP
- Above EMA
- Above consolidation H
### Targets
Primary
- Next intraday support
- PM L
- D support
- Measured move
Management target
- Trail trend using prior 2m bar.
### Management
- Respect the 2m trailing stop.
- Hold while trend structure remains intact.
- Scale only into planned levels.
- Do not exit based on unrealised P&L.
### Invalidation
Exit immediately if:
- LH fails
- Price reclaims resistance
- Buyers absorb breakdown
- Tape contradicts thesis
- Market regime changes materially
---
## Review
### Common Mistakes
- No prior down-leg.
- Shorting front-side strength.
- Entering before trigger.
- Ignoring tape absorption.
- Random profit-taking.
- Oversizing B-grade setups.

> [!warning]
> No prior down-leg → No Bouncy Ball Short.

- Trend continuation trades should be managed with the 2m trailing stop.
